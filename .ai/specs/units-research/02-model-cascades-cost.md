# Model cascades, routing, and cost-quality trade-offs — adversarial research pass

Scope: cascades/routing literature + published cost-per-task numbers on coding benchmarks, applied to
cezar's "frontier commander, cheap workers" mission design. ~14 web searches/fetches, done 2026-09-10.

## 2. Findings (numbers)

1. **FrugalGPT cascade**: matches best single LLM's (GPT-4-era) accuracy at **up to 98% lower cost**, or beats it by **+4% accuracy at equal cost**, by cascading cheap→expensive and stopping when a scorer trusts the cheap answer. Notes API pricing spans **~2 orders of magnitude** across providers — the arbitrage room the whole cascade literature exploits.
2. **RouteLLM**: a learned router between one strong and one weak model gets **up to 3.66x cost reduction** while holding quality; on individual evals, cost drop vs. always-strong was **85% on MT-Bench, 45% on MMLU, 35% on GSM8K** — i.e. savings are highly task-dependent (chat-style eval saves far more than reasoning-heavy math). Router inference overhead itself is small.
3. **Mixture-of-Agents**: a 6-proposer layered ensemble of models individually *weaker* than GPT-4 Turbo beat GPT-4 Turbo on AlpacaEval 2.0 by aggregating diverse cheap outputs; "MoA-Lite" (fewer layers, cheaper aggregator) matches GPT-4o-level quality more cheaply. Evidence that *aggregation*, not just cascading, is a second lever besides pure escalation.
4. **"Cluster, Route, Escalate" cascade** (arXiv 2606.27457, cited in a secondary blog, **not independently verified — flag low confidence**): claims keeping 97–99% of a strong model's accuracy while cutting expensive-model calls **~5x**, by escalating only the queries a quality-estimator is unsure about.
5. **SWE-bench Verified, Claude tier ladder** (Sept 2026 vendor pricing, via secondary aggregator — **medium confidence, not Anthropic's own page**): roughly Haiku-tier 73%, Sonnet-tier 85%, Opus-tier ~89%, top-tier ~95% on SWE-bench Verified, at list prices in a ~1:2:2.5:5 input-token ratio across tiers. A cited multi-turn agent task was **~2.6–3.9x cheaper on the cheap tier** than the mid tier (~$0.13 vs $0.34–0.50 for a 12-turn task) — but the cheap tier is ~12–16 points of solve-rate behind the mid tier, not free money.
6. **Aider polyglot leaderboard (primary source, 225 exercises, 6 languages)**: GPT-5(high) 88.0% correct at **$29.08/run**; DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp (Reasoner) 74.2% at **$1.30/run** — **84% of the top score for ~4.5% of the cost**; DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp (Chat, non-reasoning) 70.2% at $0.88. This is the single cleanest same-benchmark cost/quality curve found: cheap models give up ~14–18 points of accuracy for a >20x cost cut.
7. **Terminal-style agentic benchmark, cost-per-success** (Arize AI, 40 tasks, 10 models): cheapest open model `gpt-oss-120b` **$0.054/success** but only reliably solved **8/40 tasks (20–33% depending on metric)**; frontier `GPT-5.5` **$0.636/success**, solved **25–26/40 (~65%)**. **Pure routing across the model pool beat every single-model strategy on both axes at once**: $0.525/success while solving *more* tasks than any single frontier model alone — the strongest direct evidence in this pass that routing (not just cascading) is a real Pareto improvement, not just a cost/quality trade.
8. **Silent-failure cost trap** (same Arize study): a budget model (`gemini-3.5-flash`) had a 23% pass rate, and **91% of its failures (168 of 184) were simply hitting its token/turn cap** — i.e. it didn't produce a wrong answer cheaply, it burned its *entire* allotted budget producing nothing usable. Cheap models can fail in a way that costs nearly as much as a full attempt while yielding zero signal.
9. **Verifier tier matters as much as generator tier** (LangChain legal-agents case study): using **Haiku-tier as the verifier** gave a **48.4% false-pass rate per-criterion (34.7% in batch mode)** — nearly half of real failures were rubber-stamped. **DeepSeek-tier as verifier**: 10.7% false-pass (tunable to 9.5% with prompt work). Even **two frontier models cross-checking each other only agreed 95.7% of the time** — there is no free perfect verifier, only a cheaper-vs-more-reliable trade repeated one level up.
10. **Verification cost mechanics**: batch verification (scoring many criteria/items in one call) was **~100x cheaper** than per-criterion scoring with the same model, at a real accuracy cost — cost and rigor trade off *within* the verifier too, not just between generator and verifier.
11. **Price trend 2023→2026**: a cross-provider frontier-token price index is reported **84% below its March-2023 baseline** as of Sept 2026 (YipitData); frontier-class input pricing is described as **70–85% lower in 2026 than the equivalent tier in 2024**. DeepSeek V3 (Dec 2024) launched at **~$0.14/M tokens, ~1/100th of GPT-4's original price** for roughly comparable-generation capability. Frontier proprietary input pricing by 2026 sits around **$1.25–3/M tokens**, itself down sharply from 2023–24 frontier prices.

## 3. What SUPPORTS the cost-ladder hypothesis

- Every same-benchmark comparison found (Aider polyglot, SWE-bench tier ladder, Terminal-bench) shows cheap/mid models recovering **70–85% of frontier accuracy at 5–25x lower cost** — there is real, repeatable slack to exploit, not just marketing.
- Routing (RouteLLM, Arize) consistently **beats naive "always use frontier"** on cost with negligible or no quality loss when the router/estimator is decent — this is the load-bearing mechanism cezar's commander/worker split is betting on.
- Mixture-of-Agents shows aggregation of *several* cheap models can match or beat a single frontier model, not just approach it — meaning "cheap labour, frontier judgment" doesn't even need the frontier model to do the labour-equivalent work itself.
- Frontier prices are still falling fast (70–85% since 2024) — arbitrage margin between "frontier judgment" and "commodity labour" is not obviously closing; if anything the labour tier is getting cheaper *faster* than frontier, since compute-efficient open/budget models (DeepSeek, Qwen, gpt-oss-class) are the ones driving the floor down.
- FrugalGPT and RouteLLM are both from reputable labs (Zaharia/Databricks-adjacent; LMSYS) with independently reproducible open-source implementations, not vendor marketing — the core cascade result is real, not hype.

## 4. What CONTRADICTS or bounds it — be blunt

- **Savings are highly task-dependent, not universal.** RouteLLM saved 85% on chat-style MT-Bench but only 35% on GSM8K-style reasoning — the harder and more "judgment-heavy" the task, the less a router can safely divert to a cheap model. Coding agent work (cezar's actual use case) is closer to the *hard* end than to chit-chat.
- **Cheap models fail in an expensive-to-detect way, specifically for verification.** A Haiku-tier verifier had a 48.4% false-pass rate — using a cheap model to grade a cheap model's work is close to useless as a safety net. If cezar's Guard/review step uses a cheap model to review worker output, it will wave through roughly **1 in 2** real failures on the data found here. Verification quality does not scale down gracefully with cost the way generation quality does.
- **Cheap-model failures are not cheap.** The 91%-of-failures-hit-token-cap finding means a cheap worker that gets stuck burns close to its full budget for zero usable output — worse than a clean expensive success, because you then pay again for escalation/retry on top. "Cheap" is a property of a *success*, not of an *attempt*.
- **No perfect verifier exists even at the top.** Two frontier models only agreed with each other 95.7% of the time. Cezar cannot assume "have the commander review it" fully closes the gap — frontier review still has a real, non-zero miss rate; it's a risk-reduction lever, not a correctness guarantee.
- **Verification itself has its own cost/rigor trade.** Batch-scoring for cheapness costs ~10 points of false-pass accuracy vs. per-criterion scoring. A naive "add a cheap verifier pass" can silently degrade into the batch-cheap/high-false-pass regime unless someone deliberately tunes it.
- **The specific "cluster-route-escalate ~5x fewer expensive calls" figure is from a single, unverified secondary citation** — treat as a plausible order of magnitude, not a load-bearing number.
- **Headline $/task multipliers (10–24x, DeepSeek vs Claude) come mostly from list-price ratios applied to published solve rates, not from apples-to-apples third-party runs** on the same harness/date — the Aider polyglot table is the one clean exception in this pass and shows a smaller (but still large) gap: ~4.5% of the cost for 84% of the score, not literally "as good, 10x cheaper."
- **Net effect on total spend is ambiguous once escalation + verification are counted.** If a cheap worker fails on X% of tasks and each failure costs (attempt + detection + re-attempt on the escalated tier), the crossover point where cheap-first costs *more* than expensive-first is real and benchmark-dependent — none of the sources found compute this net figure directly for a commander/worker/reviewer loop; it has to be modeled or measured for cezar's own task mix.

## 5. Implications for cezar — recommended policy

1. **Route on task shape, not just task label.** Send to a cheap/local worker only tasks that look like the "easy tail" in the benchmarks above: small, well-scoped diffs (lint fixes, boilerplate, single-file bug fixes, test scaffolding, mechanical refactors) — the regime where DeepSeek/Qwen-Coder-class models recovered 70–85% of frontier accuracy. Route to a mid/frontier worker anything closer to "reasoning-heavy" (cross-file design, ambiguous spec, novel algorithm) where RouteLLM-style savings evaporate.
2. **Never let a cheap model grade its own tier's work as the sole gate.** Given the ~48% false-pass rate for a cheap verifier, the reviewer/Guard step should be at least one tier above the worker that produced the change — or, cheaper than that, gate on *objective* signals (tests pass, lints pass, diff applies, type-checks) before any LLM-based review, and reserve LLM judgment for what objective checks can't cover.
3. **Bound worker attempts hard, and treat "hit the cap" as a first-class failure mode, not a timeout to retry blindly.** Since a large share of cheap-model failures are silent budget exhaustion rather than a wrong-but-cheap answer, cezar should detect "no progress after N turns / no diff produced" and escalate immediately rather than let the worker retry into its own cap again — retrying a stuck cheap worker is the single easiest way for the ladder to become *more* expensive than going frontier-first.
4. **Escalate on a small number of concrete triggers**, mirrored from the escalation-architecture pattern found: repeated tool/test failures, invalid or non-applying diffs, no measurable progress over K iterations, or the worker itself signalling low confidence — and hand the escalated attempt the accumulated context/state rather than restarting cold (cezar's existing "autonomous nudge"/escalation-ladder mechanism, per project memory, is already the right shape for this — keep it, and make sure the units branch's cheap-worker path feeds into the same ladder rather than a separate one).
5. **Spend at least part of the savings on verification, deliberately.** The LangChain finding that batch-cheap verification trades ~10 points of false-pass accuracy for ~100x cost reduction means cezar should pick that trade explicitly per risk tier (e.g., cheap batch check for low-stakes changes, full per-criterion/frontier review before anything touching auth, payments, migrations, public API surface) rather than defaulting to whichever is cheapest everywhere.
6. **Instrument $/successful-task, not $/attempt or $/token**, per worker tier, from day one — it's the only metric in this literature that actually predicts whether the ladder is paying off, and it's cheap to compute from data cezar already has (cost per run, pass/fail from CI + review outcome).
7. **Expect the arbitrage to persist but narrow unevenly.** Frontier prices are falling 70–85%/2yr, which shrinks the multiple a commander tier costs — but the cheap-worker tier (DeepSeek/Qwen/open models) is falling at least as fast, so the *ratio* cezar is exploiting is plausibly stable-to-widening, not closing. Re-check this every 6–12 months rather than assuming it's fixed.

## 6. Confidence and gaps

- **High confidence**: the general cascade/routing thesis (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM, Mixture-of-Agents) is real, peer-reviewed-adjacent, and reproducible; the Aider polyglot leaderboard numbers (primary source, live table); the LangChain verifier false-pass numbers (named methodology, plausible and internally consistent); the qualitative direction of price declines 2023–2026.
- **Medium confidence**: the Arize $/success and silent-failure numbers (methodology described but not independently re-run here); the Claude SWE-bench tier ladder and per-task cost figures (from SEO aggregator sites reporting Sept-2026 vendor pricing, not fetched from Anthropic's own pricing page — model names like "Opus 4.8," "Sonnet 5," "Fable 5," "GPT-5.6 Sol" could not be cross-checked against a primary vendor source in this pass).
- **Low confidence, flagged in-line**: the "Cluster, Route, Escalate" ~5x figure (single secondary citation, arXiv ID not independently opened); most exact multi-turn cost-ratio numbers pulled from secondary/SEO blogs rather than vendor pricing pages or independent benchmark runs.
- **Gaps not covered in this ~14-call budget**: no direct primary-source Anthropic/OpenAI/DeepSeek pricing page was fetched (relied on aggregators); no paper specifically modeling the *net* cost of a commander→worker→reviewer loop (the crossover point where cheap-first costs more than frontier-first) was found — this is the single most decision-relevant number for cezar and appears to not exist in the literature yet; it likely has to be measured on cezar's own task distribution rather than sourced externally. Speculative-execution-style designs (cheap model drafts, expensive model verifies token-by-token) were searched for only indirectly via "verifier-gated generation" and not confirmed as directly applicable to agentic coding (most speculative-decoding literature is about token-level generation speed, not task-level agent cost, and was not pursued further given budget).
