A calmer way to work with a manager

Start from the person, not the data. Three questions matter: what do I need to decide, what is the manager doing for me, and is it going well. Each concept answers them in a few calm sentences and keeps the machinery (tasks, pull requests, runs, automations) one deliberate step away, yet within reach for a power user.

Every concept is a clickable prototype on its own page, on desktop and on a phone. Start with Today, the recommendation, then compare.

01 What every screen holds to

The current pages show everything at once: rows of chips, counts and states, and every question from every task. The person does not know where to look. These limits are the fix, and each screen was cut until it met them.

  • Lots of space. Typography instead of boxes; no cards around cards.
  • One primary action per screen.
  • At most five things visible by default.
  • No rows of chips, badges or counters, and no colour coding. One quiet dot means “something needs you”.
  • The machinery is one step away (Show the work, Everything, ⌘K), never on the first screen.
  • The three-second test: would a first-time user know where to look? If a screen needs explaining, it is too busy.

Scope

One manager, one new view next to its chat. Managers stay separate. The only cross-manager change is the Now page, because it is where the pile builds up. A wider overview and multi-manager setup come later.

The world in the prototypes

Delivery lead watches three projects: storefront, orders-api and mobile-app. It is 12:10 on a Wednesday. Fifteen things came up today: decisions, questions from tasks, approvals and failures. Names, people and PRs are invented.

02 Why Now piles up, and how it stops

Today

  • Every question, approval, failure and important message from every task and manager lands in one list, at equal weight.
  • Nobody owns the list. A question waits until you answer it or dismiss it; after seven days it just stops counting.
  • The same question arrives three times from three tasks.
  • Nothing has a default, so an unanswered question blocks its task forever.
  • Each row has its own ✕ and ⋯; Running and Finished today sit underneath. You manage the list instead of deciding.

The fix: the manager triages before you do

  1. Answer what it safely can from your rules, its memory and your earlier answers, and tell you after.
  2. Fold related questions into one decision. Three tasks asking about the payment sandbox become one question.
  3. Retire what went stale: the PR merged, the task stopped, the work moved on. Closed with a reason, never shown.
  4. Decide small, undoable things by default after a few hours, unless you object: “I will re-run last night’s tests at 17:00.” Never for merges, enabling automations or connector writes.
  5. Rank the rest by what each one unblocks, and show it one at a time.

Getting to zero feels fast

  • The count is stated as time: “Four things need you, about three minutes.”
  • One decision at a time, with one button. A thin progress line fills as you go; the next slides in.
  • Keyboard: ↵/Y, N, 1–3, M. Thumb: the full-width button sits where the thumb rests, “Not now” right under it.
  • At zero: “You’re clear. I took care of nine things for you today.” See them lists each one with its reason and a way to change it.

05 Recommendation

Build Today as the manager’s first screen, with the chat one tab away. It is the only concept that answers all three questions at a glance, and its decision component is exactly what Now needs. Borrow the best of the others:

  • From Focus: how decisions behave. One at a time, keyboard first, a count in minutes, and a proper “You’re clear”.
  • From Goals: the five lines under the decision, recurring work as ongoing goals, and per-goal settings.
  • From Quiet: the same status sentence everywhere else: the sidebar row, the notch, the phone push. It grows only when something needs you.
  • From Brief: nothing on screen. Offer it as a rule (“write me a short summary every morning”), which already exists as a saved job.
  • Control: plain rules with switches, one autonomy sentence, pause, and trust offered after your own yeses.

The tab is called Today, because “Workspace”, “Inbox” and “Now” already mean other things in cezar.

Desktop and phone differ on purpose

On a desktop you steer: the decision, the goals and the footer fit in one column, and the keyboard runs the session. On a phone you decide: the decision and its full-width button come first, goals below, and every drill-down is its own page. Approvals ask once more in a sheet, as phones do today.

06 Plan and data

Phase 1 · Decide Ship first

Rebuild Now and add Today’s top half on data that already exists:

  • One decision at a time, ranked by the attention list’s tiers, with keys and the count in minutes.
  • Stale items never shown: the settle rules already exist.
  • “Not now” uses the existing snooze overrides.
  • Plain wording from what cards already carry: the question, the proposal’s reason, the task’s outcome.
  • Today’s goals derived from what tasks reference (PR, issue, automation).

Phase 2 · The manager triages

  • Answer from rules and memory: a new close reason, “answered by the manager”, with undo.
  • Fold duplicates: cards that declare the same about merge into one.
  • Defaults: defaultAnswer and defaultAt on a question, applied by the engine unless you object. Never on approvals. Opt in per manager.
  • The “What I took care of” ledger, built from those closes.
  • Plain fields on every card: question, why, main action.

Phase 3 · Goals and rules

  • A goal record: one-time or ongoing, a state line, the tasks, PRs and automations under it.
  • Rules as sentences, each mapped to a setting, a saved job or an automation.
  • Pause with an end time, built on deactivate.
  • Trust offers: “next time, should I just do it?”, based on your approvals.
  • Holding non-urgent decisions for set check-in times.

Later: a cross-manager overview, multi-manager setup, the brief as a rule.

Kept as is: merges and enabling automations go only through the decision route, watch turns stay read-only, “no new item, no model call” still holds, nothing needs configuring, and the plain-language fields are optional (each falls back to today’s card text).

07 What we rejected, and why

A first pass put everything a manager touches on one screen: lanes of cards, job lists with trees of PRs and tasks, a control panel with every knob, and chips, counts and status dots on every row. It answered every question and none of them at a glance, and the machinery became the content. Its useful parts survive one step down: the job tree is Show the work, the control panel is How it works, and the inventory is Everything.
