What this policy covers

This policy covers CezarBot, the Cezar mobile app (called "the app" below), the relay that connects the app to a computer, and push notifications.

This policy describes the iOS and Android apps. Platform-specific storage, permissions and notification delivery are explained below.

The app is a small shell. It shows the Cezar cockpit from a Cezar server that you run or that someone runs for you. It connects in one of two ways:

  • Through the relay, after you pair the phone with Cezar on your computer.
  • Directly, when you enter the HTTPS address of a server that is reachable on the internet.

The short version

  • You do not create an account in the app. The relay asks whoever runs Cezar on a computer for an email address and to accept its terms, once per computer, in Cezar's Settings. The phone never asks for it.
  • The app contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting code, and sends no telemetry of its own.
  • Your work, such as tasks, code and messages, is encrypted end to end between the phone and your computer when you use the relay. The relay cannot read it.
  • The relay can still see some connection details, such as IP addresses, timing and data volume. These are listed below.
  • Notifications are optional and off until you turn them on for a server. The relay sends the fixed text "Cezar needs you", a computer identifier and a badge count. The phone can add the computer's saved name locally. Your tasks, code and messages are never included in the remote push payload.

What stays on your phone

The app keeps the following in its private storage. Android's backup and device transfer are turned off for this app. On iOS, the app excludes its saved-server directory, relay bundle cache and push state directory from backups. Passwords and pairing keys use device-only Keychain items, rather than iCloud Keychain sync. This does not promise that all WebKit or system-managed data is excluded from an iOS backup.

Removing a server. Forgetting a server removes its saved entry, password and cached cockpit bundle. If a push grant still needs revoking, the app keeps the pairing key and a pending revocation record until the revocation is settled or the grant expires; it retries when the app runs again.

On Android, the app clears the server's page storage and most cookies of a direct server. Cookies set for a wider domain or a sub-path, and cookies from a single sign-on provider, can stay. It does not clear the WebView's HTTP cache or a paired server's cookies, so those can stay until you clear the app's storage or uninstall it.

On iOS, the app wipes that server's WebKit data, including cookies, page storage and cache, and requests deletion of its separate data store. If WebKit cannot remove the store immediately, the app retries at the next launch. iOS Keychain items can survive uninstalling an app; CezarBot clears its old saved passwords and pairing keys on the first launch after reinstalling. Do not rely on an uninstall alone to erase Keychain data, including the shared notification names and badge counts.

Phone lock. The optional lock is off by default. Android uses its own biometric or screen-lock prompt; iOS uses Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode. The app never receives your fingerprint, face data or PIN. It only learns whether the prompt succeeded. The lock hides the app, but does not change how keys are stored. With the lock on, the app's preview is covered in the app switcher. On Android 12 and older this also blocks screenshots of the app. The iOS lock does not prevent screenshots while you use the unlocked app.

What Cezar on your computer keeps about your phone

This data is on your own computer, in ~/.cezar/relay/devices.json, and is readable only by your user account. It does not reach us through this mechanism.

  • The phone's name, taken from the phone's model name, which you can rename
  • The platform (android or ios)
  • The phone's public keys and its device ID
  • When it was added and when it was last seen
  • Push grant IDs, if you turned notifications on

You can remove a phone in Cezar's Settings. Cezar deletes the entry and tells the relay to remove the phone. If you forget a server in the app while Cezar is reachable, the entry is deleted as well. Otherwise it stays until you remove it on the computer. The phone's ID stays in the file until the relay confirms the removal.

What the relay does

The relay passes encrypted data between your phone and your computer. Nothing reaches the relay until you click Test or Connect a phone in Cezar on your computer. Your computer keeps a connection to the relay only while a phone is paired or a removal is still waiting to reach the relay.

The app's default relay is https://rome.cezar.run. If you set a different relay address, whoever runs that relay is responsible for it, and this section does not apply to them.

What the relay cannot see. Requests, responses, your code, tasks, messages, files and passwords. These are encrypted with the Noise protocol between your phone and your computer, and the relay never has the keys. It also never sees the pairing QR code.

What the relay can see while it works:

  • Your IP address and your computer's IP address
  • The public keys and identifiers used to connect: Cezar's ID, the phone's device ID and, during pairing, a one-time locator and the public keys in the handshake
  • When each side connects and disconnects, and whether the other side is online
  • How much data passes and when. Messages are padded to multiples of 256 bytes, which hides exact sizes but not overall volume
  • How many phones are connected to a computer

The relay does not store your IP address in plain form, or keep any message it forwards. It holds no undelivered messages. The relay's code writes no logs, and Cloudflare's Workers logging is off in its configuration. It reports usage data points with no ID in them: time awake, phone-connected time and the number of wake-ups. Each point covers one stretch of activity, not a total.

The relay account (email)

Since 2026-11-13 the relay asks each computer that uses it for an email address and for acceptance of the terms and privacy policy. You give them in Cezar on your computer, in Settings > Connect a phone, before the first pairing, or once when Cezar first starts after the update if phones are already paired. The app itself never asks for your email.

Why. The relay is a service run by Open Mercato. Its terms and privacy obligations require a record of who accepted the terms and a way to reach them. The email is used only to confirm that the address is yours, to send notices about the relay (such as changes to the terms, security issues or the end of the service), and to handle a deletion request. It is not used for marketing. [OPEN MERCATO TO CONFIRM: lawful basis for the account data, and that it is never used for marketing]

Emails. The relay sends one plain email per request, leading with a confirmation link, with a paragraph on why we ask. The email has no tracking pixel or tracked links. The link opens a relay page that asks for one button press, so a mail scanner that follows links cannot confirm or delete anything. If the email is not confirmed. Phones keep working for 48 hours after the computer first gives an email, or after the computer was first seen by the relay. After that the relay refuses to connect that computer's phones until the email is confirmed. Opening the link in the email reconnects them. Cezar's Settings and the phone's Confirm your email screen can only check the status and send a new link. No phone has to pair again.

What the phone sees. When the relay refuses a phone for this reason, the app asks the relay for the account's status, signed with the phone's key. The relay sends the address masked, such as p***@gmail.com, so a lost or shared phone does not reveal it, and the app shows it that way so you know where the link went. You can ask for a new link from the app; opening it and pressing the button confirms. The app takes no code and does not store the address.

Deleting the account. In Cezar on your computer, open Settings > Connect a phone and choose Delete account, or enter the address on the relay's own page at https://rome.cezar.run/account/delete, which answers the same whether or not an account exists. The relay emails a link, and following it deletes the email address, the account's list of phones and its codes, and detaches every computer from it. Deleting the account does not restart the 48-hour grace, so phones stop connecting until an email is given again.

  • Google, through Firebase Cloud Messaging, only if you turn notifications on and the build includes Firebase. It receives the push token and each notification described under Notifications.
  • Apple, through Apple Push Notification service (APNs) on iOS. The app registers with APNs at launch, before notifications are enabled.   • The Cezar server you connect to. It receives everything you do in the cockpit. If you run it, that is you. If someone else runs it, their own terms apply.

  • Hosts of images shown in the cockpit. Cockpit pages can show images stored elsewhere: GitHub profile pictures, images inside issue, pull request and tracker text, images in your agents' messages, and a project picture set by its address. Your phone loads each one directly from its host, so that host can see your phone's IP address and the time. The relay does not hide this. Whoever wrote the text chooses the host, and that can be someone you do not know.
  • Your phone's browser. Links that lead away from your server open in your browser, which has its own policy. A direct server that uses single sign-on shows that provider's login page in the app.

Telemetry

The app sends no telemetry and has no analytics or crash-reporting library. The relay's code sends none either, apart from the usage data points above.

The cockpit inside the app can report usage to Cezar on your computer. Your Cezar forwards it to Open Mercato only if you opted in. That is Cezar's own opt-in telemetry, described in Cezar's telemetry documentation. An answer to that question given on the phone is saved as your Cezar's answer.

Notifications

Notifications are off until you turn them on for a server paired through the relay. You can turn them on when the app asks after your first pairing, or with the Notifications switch on the Devices list. Android 13 and newer and iOS then ask for notification permission.

While the app is open, the app asks your Cezar for signals inside the encrypted connection and shows a notification itself. It attempts to keep this connection for up to 30 seconds after you leave; the operating system can suspend it sooner. This does not require a remote push through Apple or Google.

While the app is closed, remote delivery uses APNs (Apple) on iOS, or Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google) on Android if Firebase is included in the build and the relay is configured to deliver it:

  • On iOS the app registers with APNs at launch and holds the token in memory; it stores only a digest to detect token changes. On Android it asks Firebase for a token only after a notification switch is on. Once you enable notifications, the app sends the token to the relay. The relay seals it into a grant that only your Cezar can use, and returns the grant without storing the token. The app hands the grant to your Cezar inside the encrypted connection.
  • When Cezar needs you and the phone is not connected, Cezar asks the relay to send a push. The notification carries the fixed text "Cezar needs you", Cezar's ID and a badge count from 0 to 99, and nothing about your task titles, code, projects or messages. Apple or Google sees this payload, the token and the delivery time. The phone can replace the generic title with the saved computer name and combine badge counts locally; the saved name is not part of the remote push payload.
  • The relay keeps a revoked grant's ID for up to 90 days, so it cannot be reused, and refuses a repeated push ID for 10 minutes.
  • Turning the switch off or forgetting the server requests revocation at the relay. If it cannot complete, the app keeps the pending revocation and retries; it does not promise immediate remote revocation while offline.
  • The Android app does not ask Firebase for anything until a switch is on, and it turns off Firebase analytics. iOS uses APNs and contains no Firebase SDK.

Your choices

  • Forget a server in the app, or remove a phone on your computer. Either one stops the connection.
  • Turn the phone lock on or off.
  • Uninstalling the app removes its app-private files. On iOS, Keychain cleanup occurs on the next installation's first launch, as described above.
  • Deny the camera permission and pair by typing a code instead.
  • Turn notifications off for a server, or deny the notification permission.
  • Change or delete the relay account's email in Cezar's Settings, under Connect a phone.

The relay holds keys and IDs, the relay account's email address and acceptance records, and may receive your computer's saved name for confirmation emails.
